# Real game-code proofs and the first modernization experiments

The early recompilation work moved beyond authored examples into a small real MW3 function and a bounded game subsystem. Alongside that work, local texture, material, effects, and video experiments began exploring what optional modernization might look like.

## Recompilation progress

- Compared a real integer helper with native x64 translations across 16,609 directed and randomized cases, with no mismatches in the stated domain.
- Completed the bounded CPU and calling-convention validation milestone, including floating-point state, direct calls, shared data, and explicit handling of unsupported paths.
- Accepted an offline subsystem consisting of a buffer-state setter and its complete strided-copy consumer, checked against original behavior.

These were bounded component proofs. They did not establish a native menu, complete game startup, or playable native Training.

## Local visual research

The texture decoder and archive inventory supported the first selected texture batches. The user reviewed AI-assisted artwork, followed by material-map and animated dust, smoke, fire, and impact pilots. Local records also identified complete effects sequences and disc-video candidates for further work.

These previews explored appearance, transparency, and packaging. A promising image or decoded archive did not establish integration into every game scene, and material maps still needed renderer support.

## What came next

The runtime needed connected startup services and larger behavior comparisons. Visual experiments needed source-aware packaging, in-game checks, and clear separation from publicly distributable project code.
